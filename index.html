The Idea

A Community Album

Gale Largey conceived Life in Wellsboro not simply as a conventional history, but as a community album — a blend of photographs, newspaper accounts, historical documents, recollections, census information and the stories people remembered.

The books moved from the place — its streets, homes, churches, schools and businesses — to the people who inhabited it, then to everyday life at work and play, and finally to the main events that shaped each era.

The result was meant to be more than a record of the past. It was something families and friends could look through together, remember together, and pass to future generations.

The Third Volume
Life in Wellsboro
1960–2000

Gale Largey is returning to the story with a third Life in Wellsboro volume, carrying the community's history from 1960 through the end of the twentieth century.

This book will follow the spirit of the first two volumes: the places we remember, the people we knew, everyday life, and the events that brought Wellsboro together and changed the community over four remarkable decades.

But this part of Wellsboro's history is not hidden away waiting to be discovered in an archive. Much of it is already in our homes.

It is in family photo albums, shoeboxes of snapshots, 35mm slides, school pictures, photographs from workplaces, parades, sports teams, churches, clubs, neighborhoods, celebrations and ordinary afternoons.

And the photographs are only part of the story. We want to know who is in them, where they were taken, what was happening, and what you remember.

Wellsboro's families have the photographs. Wellsboro's people have the stories. This third volume will invite the community to bring those pieces together so they can become part of the story we leave for the generations that follow.
