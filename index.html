Life in Wellsboro

A socio-pictorial history of Wellsboro, Pennsylvania

For more than a century, Wellsboro has been photographed by the people who lived here. Those photographs — together with their names, memories and stories — preserve something that official records alone cannot: what it felt like to call Wellsboro home.

The First Two Volumes

A History Told Through Everyday Life

Life in Wellsboro, 1880–1920

Published 1980 (ASIN: B0006XWS2Y)
“This book is a delightful community album depicting life in Wellsboro between 1880 and 1920.”
Read the original sleeve notes

This book is a delightful community album depicting life in Wellsboro between 1880 and 1920. It is loaded with tidbits of interest — a fascinating blend of old photographs, newspaper accounts, excerpts from interviews with older residents, census data, and a wide array of other historical sources. It is an appreciative glimpse of Wellsboro, heartwarming in its approach.

The book’s organization is pleasantly simple to follow. First, you are presented with photographs and descriptions of the place — the streets, houses, business establishments, office buildings, the public square, churches, and so forth; then, you have an opportunity to study portraits of some of the community’s most interesting and colorful people — you’ll enjoy the faces of the people who lived in Wellsboro; next you are delighted by a series of photographs that depict everyday life — both work and play; and finally, you are given a succinct summary of the main events of the time.

You will enjoy the book many times over. It will be a ready source of conversation not only among your immediate family and friends, but also among future generations.

Life in Wellsboro, 1920–1960

Published 1988 (ASIN‎: B00071I4SC)
“LIFE IN WELLSBORO: 1920-60 is a community scrap book, a photo album, a story, a socio-historical account.”
Read the original sleeve notes

LIFE IN WELLSBORO: 1920-60 is a community scrap book, a photo album, a story, a socio-historical account. It is a blend of visual images, newspaper reports, historical documents, census data, recollections, fanciful facts, and lively anecdotes. There is no pretense that the book is anything more than a glimpse of the reality of forty years of life. Yet, in browsing through the book you are likely to experience an appreciative understanding of Wellsboro. You will more fully realize the life seasonings that have made the community such a special place.

The book is a sequel to the previous volume, LIFE IN WELLSBORO, 1880-1920. The organization is purposefully patterned in the same vein. First, one looks at the place — the streets, homes, churches, schools, business establishments, and factories in the community. In addition, there is a series of unique photos of the nearby Grand Canyon. Then, there are the people, the faces that inhabited the place, portraits which reveal the styles and expressions of the times. Next, there is a focus on the nature of the people’s activity, glimpses of them as they lived everyday life, working, playing, and praying. Finally, there is a summary of the main events of the 1920-60 period, the happenings that stirred a special flavor into everyday life.

Some images are worth a thousand words, while some words are worth a thousand images. Hopefully, the reader will discover that LIFE IN WELLSBORO, 1920-60 is rich with both images and words. Savor and enjoy it. Share it with a friend.

The Idea

A Community Album

Gale Largey conceived the Life in Wellsboro books as something more personal than a conventional history. They were designed as coffee-table-top books to be shared. Photographs, newspaper accounts, documents, recollections and everyday stories were brought together to show not simply what happened, but what life here looked and felt like.

Both of Gale’s original books followed a deliberately simple pattern: the place, the people, everyday life, and the main events of the period.

The books were made to be looked through, talked about, shared among families and friends, and remembered by generations that followed.
The Story Continues

Life in Wellsboro
1960 - 2000

George Loughery — Author
Gale Largey — Advisor
Draft cover photograph — photograph by Mark Bixby.

More than forty years after Gale Largey began documenting Wellsboro through photographs, recollections and the everyday experiences of its people, the story is continuing.

George Loughery is preparing the third volume of Life in Wellsboro, carrying the community’s story from 1960 through 2000. Gale Largey, author of the first two volumes, is serving as advisor to the project, helping provide continuity with the approach and spirit of the original books.

Like its predecessors, the new volume will look at the place, the people, everyday life, and the main events that shaped Wellsboro. But for these more recent decades, there is an extraordinary resource available: the people who lived this history and the photographs they saved.

This part of Wellsboro’s history is not hidden away waiting to be discovered in an archive. Much of it is already in our homes.

It is in family photo albums, shoeboxes of snapshots, 35mm slides, school pictures, photographs from workplaces, parades, sports teams, churches, clubs, neighborhoods, celebrations and ordinary afternoons.

And the photographs are only part of the story. We want to know who is in them, where they were taken, what was happening, and what you remember.

Wellsboro’s families have the photographs. Wellsboro’s people have the stories. The third volume will bring those pieces together so they can become part of the story we leave for the generations that follow.
